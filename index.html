Voice of customer

What customers actually say

Gathered from third-party reviews, forums and video — the site’s own pages are excluded. Every quote was verified word-for-word against the page it links to; anything that could not be verified was discarded rather than paraphrased.

Said before buying — these are conversion problems

Misleading product descriptions
Misleading information: the customer feels they were sold something different from what they got.
— wisernotify.com
Prospects make purchasing decisions based on descriptions, and if these are inaccurate, it leads to unmet expectations and distrust.
Hidden or unexpected fees
The user is enticed with a low advertised price. After investing time and effort, they discover unexpected fees and charges when they reach the checkout.
— deceptive.design
Prospects are confused and frustrated by prices that increase unexpectedly during the checkout process, making it hard to compare and trust.
Difficulty comparing true costs
Hidden fees deter customers from making accurate pricing comparisons. This results in a higher likelihood of making “wrong” shopping choices.
— shawlewenz.com
Undisclosed fees prevent prospects from accurately comparing products or services, leading to potentially suboptimal purchasing decisions.
Need for social proof and trust
I read reviews about tools because I want to know if they are user-friendly, have bugs, or have helpful customer support. I’d like to know about the features, pricing, and perhaps, the platform's reliability. In other words, I need to read other users' experiences and decide if it’s worth it.
— famewall.io
Prospects rely on peer experiences and reviews to assess product quality, usability, and support before committing to a purchase, reducing perceived risk.
Unclear refund or return policies
If your refund policy is unclear, it can harm customer experience and make your roadmap for processing requests difficult.
— squarespace.com
Ambiguous refund or return policies create uncertainty and risk for prospects, potentially deterring them from making a purchase.
